# GRA — Gravity Reputation Architecture: an overview for partners

> **Status: research framework in development, evolving into a methodology.**
> This page is written for universities and professional organisations. The
> full documentation is in Russian; the structure is described in
> [docs/README.md](README.md).

## In one paragraph

GRA is an author's research framework for analysing reputational, social and
information relationships. It treats a reputational landscape as a network of
actors. The position of each actor depends not only on its own attributes but
also on the structure, intensity and context of its ties. GRA borrows the
*language* of physics — mass, distance, polarity, fields, gravity, magnetism —
to formulate testable hypotheses about that structure. It does not claim that
physical laws operate in society.

## What GRA is and is not

| GRA is | GRA is not |
| --- | --- |
| A conceptual framework with explicit constructs, hypotheses and a test procedure | A validated method, a standard or a technology (not yet) |
| A **complement** to established reputation and communication measurement practice | An alternative to RepTrak, the AMEC Integrated Evaluation Framework, the Barcelona Principles or ISO 20671 |
| A model for **collecting, classifying, structuring and storing** relationship data with provenance | A decision-making system or a business-performance optimisation method |
| Open to falsification: every claim has a status and a falsification condition | A set of proven results |

## Constructs

| Construct | Working definition | Scientific lineage GRA builds on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reputation mass | An actor's reputational weight: attributes plus network position. Distinct from influence on a particular decision | Network centrality (Freeman 1978), PageRank, reputational capital (Fombrun 1996) |
| Distance | Multidimensional separation: physical, communicational, social/trust, value-based, institutional | Proximity framework (Boschma 2005) |
| Polarity | Sign and character of a tie (+1, −1, 0); its direction is a separate hypothesis | Structural balance and signed networks (Heider 1946; Cartwright & Harary 1956; Leskovec et al. 2010) |
| Information fields | Media and communication environments that change effective distance and interaction intensity | Two-step flow (Katz & Lazarsfeld 1955), diffusion of innovations |
| Gravity (model) | Attraction towards centres of reputational weight | Gravity models (Stewart 1948; Tinbergen 1962), radiation model (Simini et al. 2012) |
| Magnetism (model) | Mutual attraction or repulsion depending on compatibility, interests and relationship character | Homophily (McPherson et al. 2001) |

The novelty claim is limited and testable. GRA looks at these components
*together* and applies them to reputation practice, with explicit evidence
statuses. No single component is claimed to be new.

## Evidence discipline

- Every statement is labelled ● fixed, ◐ interpretation or ○ hypothesis.
  "Unknown" is recorded as such, never as zero.
- Every fact carries provenance (source, date, quote). Conflicting sources are
  marked VERIFIED, PARTIAL or CONFLICT, not averaged.
- Hypotheses are tested against a baseline (reach + sentiment) and at least
  one competing operationalisation, with the falsification condition stated
  before data collection.
- Outputs of AI agents count as preliminary checks, never as evidence, until
  they are independently verified.
- Only open, public data sources are used.

## Open research questions

1. Does relationship structure explain decision outcomes better than reach and
   sentiment?
2. How many distance dimensions are needed, and are the physical and
   communicational dimensions independent?
3. How can the sign and direction of ties be observed reliably?
4. How can mass be operationalised without collapsing it into influence?
5. Which functional form fits best: the founder's gravity-type formula,
   estimated exponents, a radiation-type model or an ordinal model?
6. How does magnetism differ empirically from gravity?

Concept → **Conceptual framework (current)** → Methodology → Validated
methodology → Technology / de-facto standard. Each step has an explicit gate
criterion ([roadmap.md](roadmap.md)).

## Working with us

There are no partnerships yet. We are looking for:

1. **Academic partners** to review the constructs and co-design studies on one
   question (for example, distance dimensionality or structure vs. baseline).
2. **Professional organisations** to check the framework against established
   measurement and evaluation practice.
3. **Practitioners with data** to provide cases with known outcomes.

We do not publish indices or rankings based on GRA before the constructs are
operationalised. Any cooperation is subject to legal review of the applicable
restrictions and data-protection rules.
